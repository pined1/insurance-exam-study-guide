I

Basic Insurance Concepts & Principles

🎧
Listen~1.2 min

1. Definition of Insurance (CIC 22)

💡
Insurance = transfer of risk of loss from an individual/business to an insurer, which spreads the cost among many insureds. Without insurance, the loss falls solely on whoever suffered it.

Statutory definition: insurance is "a contract whereby one undertakes to indemnify another against loss, damage, or liability arising from a contingent or unknown event."

Person (legal) = an entity acting on its own behalf, accepting legal/civil responsibility, contracting in its own name — individuals, associations, organizations, corporations, partnerships, and trusts.

2. Insurable Events (CIC 250)

"Any contingent or unknown event, whether past or future, which may damnify a person having an insurable interest, or create a liability against him, may be insured against."

⚠️
Predictability rule: the more predictable a loss, the more insurable it becomes; the more unpredictable, the less insurable. Gambling/lottery losses = NOT insurable.
⚠️
The law limits only the TYPE of insurable event — never the dollar amount. Level of indemnification is set by the parties in the contract.

3. Reinsurance (CIC 620)

One insurer indemnifies another for part/all of its liabilities. Purpose: protection against catastrophic losses.

Ceding insurerThe original company (it cedes/gives away the risk)
Assuming insurerThe reinsurer (takes on the risk)

  • Earthquake appears under BOTH Fire and Miscellaneous.

Contract Law

🎧

1. The Insurance Policy (CIC 380)

The insurance policy = the written instrument setting forth the contract of insurance.

2. Six Required Specifications (CIC 381)

#Element

⚠️

3. Key Legal Terms & Distinctions

⚠️
Key distinctions to memorize:
  • Fraud = misrepresentation that is both material AND intentional. Misrepresentation alone ≠ fraud.
  • Concealment can void a policy even if unintentional — unlike fraud.
  • A representation can be withdrawn/changed only before the policy takes effect — not after.
  • CIC 354: a representation cannot qualify an express warranty, but CAN qualify an implied warranty.
  • Neither party must disclose personal opinions/judgment — only facts.
Warranty Details (CIC 440–449)
  • No special wording needed to create a warranty.
  • Express warranty = written in policy → automatically part of contract.
  • Implied warranty = unwritten, presumed from circumstances.
  • Created at or before execution; not limited by time (past, present, future, or combination).
Concealment — what does NOT need to be disclosed (CIC 330–339)
  • Known information / information that should be known
  • Information the other party waives
  • Information excluded by a warranty and not material to the risk
  • Information excepted from insurance and not material to the risk
  • Information based on personal judgment
Materiality (CIC 334)
💡
Disadvantages are always material information. Producers must disclose disadvantages (surrender charges, exclusions like war/terrorism/aviation/suicide, term length, premium increases, fees, substandard ratings) — not just advantages.

Example: concealing a recent heart attack has greater materiality impact than misstating age by 5 years — materiality is about impact on the decision, not the type of fact.

Representations & Misrepresentations (CIC 350–361, 780–784)

A representation is false when the facts fail to correspond with its assertions. A material misrepresentation would alter the underwriting decision if discovered. If material AND intentional → fraud.

4. Rescission (CIC 331, 338, 359, 447)

An injured party may rescind for:

  1. A false material representation (effective from when the representation became false)
  2. Concealment (regardless of intent)
  3. Violation of a material warranty or other material provision

Burden of proof: insurer must prove the omission was intentional AND done to obtain insurance fraudulently.

All claim/notice forms must carry CA's fraud-warning statement — except reinsurance policies (insurer-to-insurer transactions).

Taking both a client fee AND an insurer commission on one deal = potentially unethical.

2. Life Settlements

Life settlement = selling a no-longer-needed policy to a third party for cash. Seller's life expectancy must be more than 1 year (distinguishes from viatical settlements, for the terminally ill).

TermDefinition
Qualified Institutional BuyerOwns/invests ≥$100 million in securities; SEC-permitted to trade unregistered securities

⚠️
NOT a life settlement contract: policy loans, bank loans, collateral assignments, agreements between close relatives, bona fide business succession, employer-owned key-employee insurance, service-recipient/provider agreements.
🔢
Licensing: 15 hrs CE + $171 fee (1-yr) — OR, if already a life agent ≥1 year: notify CDI within 10 days, pay $85 notification fee, renew biennially.

3. Acting Without Appointment

  • Insurer requires all agents appointed (captive) → unappointed agent can't solicit/submit applications.
  • Insurer doesn't require it → unappointed agent can present proposals; client payments go to the insurer, not agent; if a policy issues, insurer is deemed to have appointed the agent and must file notice with the Commissioner within 14 days.

4. Solicitors

  • Assists a P&C agent/broker with non-life/disability/health insurance.
  • Cannot simultaneously hold an agent/broker license.
  • Can work for multiple producers at once.
⚠️
"Life solicitor" and "accident & health solicitor" are NOT real licenses (CIC 1704) — heavily tested.

5. Agent's Errors & Omissions (E&O) Insurance

  • Covers negligent errors (done wrong) or omissions (not done). Highest-risk moments: sales interview & policy delivery.
  • Claims-made (modern trend) vs. occurrence (older, requires insured's consent for out-of-court settlement).
  • Deductibles usually $500–$1,000 per claim; renews annually.
⚠️
Not covered: criminal acts, unfair trade practices, material misrepresentation causing loss. If a claim results in a criminal conviction, E&O won't pay; agent stays personally liable.

Nonadmitted Insurers, Records, License Lifecycle & Ethics

🎧
Listen~1.2 min

1. Nonadmitted Insurers & Surplus Lines (CIC 703)

Nonadmitted insurer = not licensed/present in CA; represented only via surplus lines brokers.

🔢
Unlicensed acts on behalf of a nonadmitted insurer = misdemeanor + $500 fine + $100/month continuing violation.

2. Free Insurance Ban (CIC 777.1)

Illegal to give free insurance as an incentive for other business → up to 1-year suspension/revocation.

Exceptions: newspaper subscriptions, credit union shares, product-performance guarantees, title/life/disability insurance paying off debt on death/disability, attorney services, motor club services (towing, roadside, bail bond, DMV).

4. Fiduciary Duty & MGAs

Diverting fiduciary funds = theft. Producer must remit premiums (minus commission) and hold funds in a separate trustee account.

MGA = negotiates ceding reinsurance / manages insurance business as insurer's agent.

⚠️
NOT an MGA: insurer's employee, US manager of an alien insurer's branch, underwriting manager not paid on premium volume, attorney-in-fact for a reciprocal insurer.

5. Appointment Rules

  • Insurer must file notice of appointment within 14 days; licensee becomes legal / insurer becomes liable as of the date the insurer signs.
  • Inactive license (no appointments) ≠ lost license — reactivates on next appointment.
  • Licensee may surrender license anytime (CIC 1708).

6. License Denial / Suspension / Termination

💡
No-hearing denial (exactly 4 conditions): felony conviction, misdemeanor insurance-law violation, license denial in past 5 yrs, license suspension/revocation in past 5 yrs.
  • "Convicted" includes nolo contendere (no contest) pleas.
  • All convictions must be disclosed, even if dismissed/expunged/probated — failure to disclose = automatic denial.
  • Cheating on the license exam → barred from exams/active license for 5 years.
  • License auto-terminates on death, org dissolution, or partner changes (unless filed within 30 days).

7. Continuing Education

  • 24 hours per 2-year term, incl. 3 hours ethics (Life, A&H, Property, Casualty, Personal Lines).
  • Multi-license holders: one set of CE satisfies all held license types.
  • Carryover: only 2nd-year hours carry over, capped at the renewal requirement.
  • Exempt: 30 continuous years licensed in CA and age 70+.
  • LTC training: 8 hrs/year for first 4 years, then 8 hrs/2-year term (counted within, not added to, overall CE).

8. Ethics

Client's interest first; competence; needs-based recommendations; plain language; confidentiality; obey the law; no unfair competitor remarks.

⚠️
Conflict of interest: recommending what benefits the agent's commission over the client, or what the agent wouldn't choose for themselves.
Prohibited: misrepresentation, twisting, concealment, diverting/commingling client funds (commingling is a violation regardless of intent).

Highest-Yield Trap Summary

🎧
Listen~0.9 min

  • Fraud (material + intentional) vs. concealment (no intent needed) vs. misrepresentation (neither required, but must be material to void).
  • Fraud penalties: $150K/felony (Penal Code §550) vs. $25K/misdemeanor (CIC 782) — don't swap these.

  • Liability insurance excludes: workers comp, common carrier, boiler & machinery, team & vehicle.
  • Ocean marine (by sea) vs. inland marine (land/air + pleasure watercraft hull).

  • Surplus line broker is the only license type allowed to place business with nonadmitted insurers.
  • The four no-hearing license denial conditions: felony conviction, misdemeanor insurance-law violation, license denial in past 5 yrs, suspension/revocation in past 5 yrs.
  • Domicile = place of incorporation, never place of business.
  • Stock/nonparticipating (taxable stockholder dividends) vs. Mutual/participating (nontaxable policyowner dividends).

I

Policy Replacement & Cancellation

🎧
Listen~1.8 min

1. Free-Look / Right to Cancel

Every individual life policy or annuity must state on the cover page/policy that the owner can return it within a set period for a full refund, no questions asked. Rescission means the policy is treated as if it never existed.

⚠️
Clock starts when the policy is received and signed for by the owner (Acknowledgment of Delivery Receipt) — NOT when the application was signed or the policy was underwritten.
TriggerFree-look periodRefund
Standard individual life policyAt least 10 days; insurer may extend up to 30 days (never less than 10)Full premium refund
Replacement policyMinimum 30 daysFull refund
Insured/annuitant age 60+30 daysFull refund
Variable life/annuity30 daysAccount value + policy fees (could be less than premium paid)
Insurer's deadline to refund after rescission notice—Must pay within 30 days
⚠️
Traps:
  • Free-look does NOT apply to term conversions or credit life policies.
  • The 10–30 day range is for standard policies; 30 days flat is required for replacements and age 60+ policies.
  • Variable products refund account value (can be less than premiums paid) — non-variable refunds are the full premium.
  • After the free-look period expires, cancellation may trigger a surrender charge (not automatically refundable).
Age 60+ Notice Requirement
  • Must appear on cover page/policy jacket in 12-point bold, with 1-inch margin on all sides, or on an affixed sticker.
  • Applies to individual life policies/annuities — excludes variable contracts and modified guaranteed contracts.
  • The phrase "known as a surrender charge" may be omitted if no surrender charges exist.

2. Replacement Rules (CIC 10509–10509.09)

Replacement = purchasing a new individual life/annuity contract that causes an existing policy with another insurer to be lapsed, surrendered, terminated, forfeited, converted (e.g., a nonforfeiture option), or have benefits reduced.

⚠️

Memorize these time limits separately:
  • 20 days — existing insurer's conservation outline
  • 5 working days — existing insurer furnishing annuity summary/ledger
  • 30 days — free-look on replacement policies AND age-60+ policies
  • 3 years — record retention (not 2, not 5)
Replacement Rules Do NOT Apply To:
  • Credit life insurance
  • Group life insurance or group annuities
  • Same-insurer conversions/changes (internal policy change)
  • Replacing insurer = existing insurer (same-company transaction)
No-Agent Sales (direct response/internet)
  • Insurer must still present the replacement disclosure as soon as replacement is suspected, even if that means at policy delivery (e.g., via mail).
  • Must still ask on the application about existing life insurance/replacement intent.
  • Not using agents does not excuse replacement duties.

Burden of compliance falls on the replacing insurer — must ensure its agents comply, and must return incomplete applications/disclosures to the agent.

3. Agent Duty & Liability Pitfalls

⚠️
Never leave a client without coverage. If a client cancels/lapses the old policy before the new one is approved and then gets declined or rated on the new application, the client may become uninsurable.
  • If a client insists on canceling early against advice: get a signed letter stating the client is acting against the agent's advice (protects the agent from liability).

Replacement considerations to weigh (common distractor topics):

  • New policy may cost more (client is older, potentially less insurable)
  • Incontestability period may restart with the new insurer — check if the new company honors the already-met period
  • Premium guarantees may differ between policies
  • Surrender charges on the current cash-value policy — consider a 1035 exchange

4. Penalties for Replacement Violations

⚠️
Commissioner may suspend or revoke license of any violator. Trap: insurer penalties run roughly 10× the agent penalties at each tier — easy to swap the numbers.

5. Required Cover-Page Disclosures (CIC 10127.10)

Non-variable individual life/annuity policies need the standard 30-day/surrender-charge notice, in 12-pt bold, with 1-inch margins, on the cover page/jacket or a sticker.

⚠️
Excludes: variable contracts, modified guaranteed contracts, credit-transaction policies, contractual conversion/policy-change provisions, contributory/noncontributory employer group life or annuity contracts, and group term life.

Variable annuity/life/modified guaranteed contracts require an expanded notice covering: cancellation policy, investment restrictions, refund policy, and penalty/surrender charge.

6. Investment Handling During Free-Look (CIC 10127.10)

During the 30-day free-look for a variable annuity, the premium defaults to fixed-income/money-market investments unless the purchaser specifically directs it into the stock/bond portfolio underlying the contract.

⚠️
Refund consequence depends on where the money was placed:
  • Left in fixed/money-market (default) and returned within 30 days → refund of premium + policy fees (full).
  • Directed into stock/bond portfolio and returned within 30 days → refund of account value on the date received by the insurer/agent — could be less than the premium paid.
Biggest trap here: the variable-product refund is conditional on the investment choice, not automatically the full premium — opposite of the standard non-variable free-look, which always refunds the full premium.
II

Insurable Interest

🎧
Listen~1.6 min

Core Definition

💡
Insurable interest = a legitimate financial/relational stake in the continued life of the insured, required so insurance can't become a wager on someone's death. Insurance indemnifies; it does not allow speculation on another's life.
  • Anyone can insure their own life — no insurable interest issue (the owner controls the policy: increase, decrease, cancel, change provisions).
  • To insure someone else's life, you need insurable interest and that person's consent.

The 4 Test Questions

  1. Would the insured's death cause the policyowner (or dependents) financial hardship?
  2. Does the insured owe the policyowner money or economic support (current/future)?
  3. Do they share a close relationship with common economic/familial goals?
  4. Do they have common ownership interest in property?

Statutory List — Every Person Has Insurable Interest In:

#Interest
1Himself/herself
2Any person he/she depends on wholly or in part for education or support
3Any person under legal obligation for payment of money/property/services that could be delayed/prevented by death or illness (e.g., child support)
4Any person upon whose life an estate or interest vested in him/her depends

Memory Device: "Blood and Business"

CategoryIncludes
BloodFamily relations — spouse, parent/child, siblings; support one another or share common goals (e.g., home/business purchase)
BusinessBusiness partners/co-owners, employer–key employee, creditor–debtor (lender/borrower)
⚠️
"Blood" also covers former family relations — e.g., divorce decrees requiring the paying spouse to maintain life insurance on themself for alimony/child support. Ownership structure: the receiving spouse owns the policy on the paying spouse's (insured's) life.

Insurable Interest vs. Beneficiary Designation

⚠️
  • Insurable interest applies only to the policyowner, NOT the beneficiary.
  • Neither the policyowner nor the insured must show insurable interest when naming a beneficiary.
  • A beneficiary can be anyone — a stranger, business, or charity — as long as they're willing to accept the money.

Applies across insurance types generally (e.g., you can't insure a car you don't own — same ownership-interest logic).

⏰ Timing Rule — Highest-Yield Exam Trap

Insurance TypeWhen must insurable interest exist?
Life & HealthAt the time of application ONLY — not required to exist at the time of loss (death) (CIC 10110)
Property & Casualty (P&C)At both the time of application AND the time of loss
💡
This is the single biggest life-vs-P&C distinction tested: a life beneficiary's/owner's insurable interest can lapse (e.g., divorce) after issue and the policy remains valid, since interest existed at application. P&C coverage would not pay out if ownership interest is gone at the time of the loss.

Application note: if the relationship between insured and proposed owner isn't obvious (e.g., different last name/address), the agent should note the relationship on the application so the underwriter can verify insurable interest exists.

Highest-Yield Trap Summary

🎧

  • Free-look clock starts on delivery/signed receipt, never on application-signing or underwriting.

II

California Standard Fire Insurance Policy

🎧
Listen~2.3 min

The mandated core policy form underlying CA fire coverage. Exam questions pull from its exact clauses.

1. Concealment & Fraud Clause

The entire policy is void if the insured willfully concealed or misrepresented any material fact/circumstance concerning the insurance, the subject of it, or the insured's interest — or committed fraud or false swearing — whether before or after a loss.

⚠️
Nuance vs. Ch. 1 §II: general CIC concealment doctrine voids a policy regardless of intent. This standard-fire-policy clause specifically says "willfully" — i.e., this particular clause targets intentional concealment, and it uniquely applies both before and after a loss.

2. Uninsured & Excepted Property

The policy does not cover accounts, bills, currency, deeds, evidences of debt, money, or securities; nor bullion or manuscripts unless specifically named in writing.

3. Perils Not Included

  • Enemy attack, invasion, insurrection, rebellion, revolution, civil war, or usurped power
  • Order of any civil authority — except destructive acts taken to prevent the spread of fire
  • Neglect of the insured to use all reasonable means to save/preserve the property
  • Theft
⚠️
"Order of civil authority" is excluded except when the destruction was to stop a fire from spreading — that specific case IS covered.

4. Other Insurance, Suspension & Endorsements

  • Other insurance may be prohibited or limited by endorsement.
  • Coverage is suspended while: the hazard is increased within the insured's control/knowledge; the building is vacant/unoccupied for 60+ consecutive days; or loss results from explosion/riot — unless fire ensues, in which case only the fire loss is covered.
  • Any additional peril must be added by written endorsement.
  • No waiver of any provision is valid unless it's in writing.
🔢
60 consecutive days vacant/unoccupied = coverage suspension trigger.

5. Insured's Duties After a Loss

  • Give written notice without unnecessary delay; protect property from further damage; separate damaged from undamaged property; furnish a complete inventory.
  • Render a signed, sworn proof of loss within 60 days after the loss, unless the insurer extends it in writing.
  • May be required to exhibit remaining property and submit to examination under oath.
  • Insurer must inform the insured that tax returns are privileged; must furnish copies of claim-related documents within 15 calendar days of a request (excluding privileged/attorney work-product material).
🔢
60 days — proof of loss deadline. 15 calendar days — insurer's deadline to produce claim-related documents once requested.

  • The company may elect to take all/part of the property at the agreed/appraised value, or to repair/rebuild/replace with like kind and quality — it must notify its intent within 30 days after receiving the proof of loss.

  • Suit for recovery must be commenced within 12 months after the loss, or it's barred.

🎧
Listen~2.1 min

(CIC 125–140; Federal Liability Risk Retention Act, USC Title 15)

1. Core Definitions

TermDefinition
Risk Retention Group (RRG)A corporation/LLC whose primary activity is assuming & spreading all or part of its group members' liability exposure; doesn't offer insurance other than liability insurance; members share related business/activities; name must include "risk retention group."

2. Purpose of the Federal Liability Risk Retention Act

  • Regulate the formation and operation of RRGs and PGs in California.
  • Help Californians who are having difficulty obtaining liability coverage form and operate RRGs/PGs.
  • Authorize RRG formation for directors and officers of corporations (for-profit or nonprofit) in the same line of business.

3. Regulatory & Tax Treatment

  • An RRG is liable for premium taxes on CA business (gross premiums written, less returned premiums) and is taxed/fined like a foreign admitted insurer.
  • Surplus line brokers must report to the Commissioner premiums placed with an RRG not chartered in California.
  • RRGs must comply with CA's deceptive/fraudulent practice laws, but the Commissioner needs a court injunction to act on that conduct.
  • An RRG must submit to a Commissioner-requested exam if its domicile regulator hasn't examined it within 60 days of California's request.

4. Mandatory Disclosure & Restrictions

💡
Every RRG application and policy must carry this notice: "NOTICE: This policy is issued by your risk retention group. Your risk retention group may not be subject to all of the insurance laws and regulations of your state. State insurance insolvency guarantee funds are not available for your risk retention group."
  • RRGs can't solicit/sell to anyone ineligible for membership, or while in a hazardous financial condition.
  • RRGs can't join or contribute to any CA insurance insolvency guarantee fund — and their insureds aren't eligible for guarantee-fund benefits.
  • RRGs are excluded from CA's joint underwriting associations, the CA Automobile Assigned Risk Plan, the FAIR Plan, and market assistance plans.
  • A PG buying from a nonadmitted insurer or an RRG must tell its members the risk isn't protected by a guarantee fund and may not follow all CA insurance laws.

5. Who Can Place This Business

  • Only a licensed fire & casualty broker-agent may procure RRG liability insurance in CA — except salaried RRG employees/officers not paid on a commission basis.
  • Only a licensed surplus line broker may procure PG liability insurance from a nonadmitted insurer on behalf of a CA purchasing group (a nonresident may hold this license for that purpose).
  • No civil liability attaches to an agent/broker who placed liability coverage for a properly licensed/incorporated CA RRG that later becomes insolvent.

I

Standard Fire Policy — History & Property Coverage

🎧
Listen~1.3 min

1. Origins of the Standard Fire Policy

The Standard Fire Policy (SFP) was drafted by the insurance industry in 1918 to indemnify the insured for loss or damage to the insured building, furniture, fixtures, or other named personal property, caused by fire.

In 1943, it was standardized into the "New York Standard Fire Policy" and adopted by 46 of the 48 states at the time as the standard form. (Massachusetts adopted the New England Fire Policy; Texas adopted its own form.)

The policy set out its conditions in a 165-line format and required additional forms to be attached to address important aspects of coverage.

💡
The SFP became the basis for property coverage on both personal and commercial policies. ISO's simplified-language policies in the 1980s phased the SFP itself out of day-to-day use, but its conditions and provisions are still incorporated into most property policies today. Some states require property policies to contain language at least equal to the SFP's.

2. Property Covered & Excluded

The 1943 standardization added coverage for fire, lightning, and the removal of property after a fire (to protect undamaged property from further damage). Modern policies also cover property removed because of endangerment by an insured peril, even though a loss has not yet occurred.

Property covered is limited to property actually described in the policy, for loss by a peril insured against in the policy.

The Removal Provision

If described property is removed from its original location to protect it from an insured peril, the policy automatically covers that property for 5 days at the other location against virtually all types of perils (all-risk).

⚠️
Don't confuse the two coverage types: the base policy is named-peril only, but the removal provision temporarily upgrades removed property to all-risk for 5 days — a peril otherwise excluded (like theft) is covered during that window.

Example: Joe's building and contents are insured. The building catches fire, so Joe removes the contents and puts them in a warehouse so they won't burn. That night, the removed contents are stolen. Even though theft is NOT a covered peril under the policy, Joe would still collect for the loss under the removal provision.

Even though the SFP identifies covered property generally, an additional coverage form must still be attached to actually describe the type of property being covered. Manuscripts or bullion are covered only if specifically named in the policy.

3. Excluded Property

The Standard Fire Policy excludes coverage for:

  • Accounts
  • Currency
  • Deeds
  • Bills
  • Evidence of debt, money, or securities
II

Standard Fire Policy — Perils, Losses & Limitations

🎧
Listen~2.0 min

1. Insuring Agreement — Three Named Perils

The SFP covers direct loss resulting only from:

  1. Fire — hostile fires only, not friendly fires
  2. Lightning
  3. Removal of property from premises endangered by an insured peril — automatic for 5 days
💡
The SFP is a named-peril contract — it does not cover any peril not listed in the insuring agreement, though numerous endorsements exist to add or increase the perils covered.

2. Losses Covered & Excluded

Without endorsement, the 165-line 1943 NY Standard Fire Policy covers loss/damage on an ACV basis by:

PerilCoverage detail
FireNot defined in the SFP itself; courts define it as "oxidation sufficiently rapid to cause a flame or glow." Friendly fire (deliberately ignited, stays within its intended confines — e.g., a fireplace) is NOT covered. Hostile fire (escapes its intended confines) is covered.
LightningDamage from natural electricity is covered; damage from artificially generated electricity is not covered, unless it results in a hostile fire — then only the fire damage is covered.
Removal5 days of coverage at another location; while there, the property is insured against all types of perils (all-risk) on a pro-rata basis.
⚠️
Claims for charring or scorching alone, with no flame or glow, are often denied — that's not legally "fire" under the SFP's judicial definition.

Only direct losses caused by the named perils are covered — direct loss means actual physical damage to, or destruction of, the insured property.

Specifically Excluded Losses (Directly or Indirectly Caused)
  • Any act of war
  • Ordinances of civil authority
  • Insured's neglect to reasonably save or preserve the property
  • Theft
  • Loss occurring while the hazard is increased within the knowledge or control of the insured
  • Any loss occurring when the building is vacant or unoccupied for 60 days or more

3. Limitations on Amount Payable

To prevent overinsurance, the amount payable cannot exceed the smallest of:

LimitMeaning
Policy limitThe amount stated in the policy
Actual cash value (ACV)Replacement cost minus depreciation, at time of loss
Cost to repair or replaceWith property of like kind and quality, within a reasonable time
Interest of the insuredThe amount equal to the insured's interest in the property at the time of loss
Pro rata liabilityLimits the insurer's liability to a portion of the loss no greater than the amount the insured could collect from all insurers covering the property, whether actually collected or not

Example: a $100,000 building is insured under two separate standard fire policies of equal amount. Without the pro rata clause, the insured might try to collect $200,000 for a total loss of a $100,000 building; with the clause, each insurer pays only its pro rata share (half, in this example).

4. California Regulations (CIC 2070)

All fire policies issued in California must be written in the standard form and may not contain any additions. No part of the standard form may be omitted or changed, except for a policy providing coverage against the peril of fire only (or fire combined with other perils), provided the fire coverage is substantially equivalent to or more favorable to the insured than the standard form.

🔢
Don't mix these up: 5 days = automatic all-risk removal coverage at another location. 60 days or more = vacancy/unoccupancy exclusion trigger. Different provisions, different purposes.
III

Residential Property Insurance Disclosure

🎧
Listen~1.8 min

1. Who Must Receive It (CIC 10101, 10102)

For all policies written or renewed in California, the named insured must be given a copy of the California Residential Property Insurance Disclosure Statement. The agent or insurer must obtain the applicant's signature acknowledging receipt of the disclosure form within 60 days of the date of the application, beginning at the time of the original application.

The Disclosure Statement describes the principal forms of dwelling insurance coverage available in California and identifies which form of coverage the insured purchased or selected. It is only a general description and is NOT part of the actual policy.

2. Building Code Upgrade Coverage

Building code upgrade coverage disclosed by the form applies only to the extent that codes, standards, or laws impose stricter requirements than the level of insurance coverage applicable to the property.

If the policy includes this coverage, the insurer must: (1) state on the declarations page, in no less than 10-point type, that the coverage is included, state any applicable limits, and identify any other terms/conditions/restrictions there or in a separate attached disclosure form; and (2) if a separate disclosure form is used for those terms/limits/restrictions, print it in no less than 10-point type.

3. No-Fire-Coverage Rule

If an insurer issues a new residential property policy on or after July 1, 2021 that does not cover the peril of fire, the insurer must: (1) obtain a signed acknowledgment from the applicant/insured that the new policy doesn't cover fire; and (2) prominently disclose on the declarations page a bold, uppercase statement in no less than 12-point type, plus information on the FAIR Plan and the CA Insurance Finder.

⚠️
Required wording: "THIS POLICY DOES NOT COVER THE PERIL OF FIRE. THERE ARE OTHER RESOURCES FOR FINDING FIRE COVERAGE, INCLUDING USING THE CALIFORNIA DEPARTMENT OF INSURANCE FIRE COVERAGE FINDER OR PURCHASING COVERAGE FROM THE CALIFORNIA FAIR PLAN ASSOCIATION."

4. Coverage-Level Hierarchy

💡
Actual Cash Value coverage = the most limited level of coverage listed on the disclosure. Guaranteed Replacement Cost = the broadest level of coverage.

Government Pools & Catastrophe Coverage

🎧
Listen~2.0 min

1. Earthquake Coverage (CIC 10081, 10084)

Earthquake = a trembling or shaking of the earth of volcanic or seismic origin, often resulting in severe damage. It's a peril excluded by most standard property forms; coverage may be added by endorsement, or written as a separate Difference in Conditions (DIC) policy.

Every residential property policy issued, delivered, or initially renewed in California must include an offer of earthquake coverage — either within the residential policy itself (by provision or endorsement), or in a separate policy/certificate covering earthquake alone or combined with other perils.

Insurers may comply by: (1) offering to underwrite the risk directly; (2) arranging for coverage through an affiliated insurer; or (3) arranging for coverage through an agent/broker under a policy issued by a nonaffiliated insurer.

2. California Earthquake Authority (CEA) (CIC 10089.6, 10089.26, 10089.28)

The CEA is a publicly managed, not-for-profit organization administered under the Commissioner's authority. It is authorized to sell policies of basic residential earthquake insurance only — it may not sell any other line of insurance. Participating insurers write the underlying residential property policy and may be reasonably compensated for claims and policyholder services provided on the CEA's behalf.

  3. Premiums may be surcharged up to an additional 20% if CEA's obligations rise to a predefined level.

3. California FAIR Plan (CIC 10090, 10091, 10093a, 10091c)

FAIR = Fair Access to Insurance Requirements. It provides equitable distribution among admitted insurers of the responsibility for insuring qualified property that cannot get basic property insurance through the normal marketplace.

The FAIR Plan is an industry placement facility and joint reinsurance association — not a state agency, even though the state established it. All CA property insurers participate, proportional to market share (a company writing 10% of CA's property insurance underwrites 10% of every FAIR Plan policy).

⚠️
The FAIR Plan is often mistaken for a state agency or for having its own agent force — it's neither. It has no agents of its own; agents/brokers cannot bind or commit the FAIR Plan. All business is placed on a brokerage basis: the producer represents the insured, not the FAIR Plan.

A producer must be admitted by the FAIR Plan association to submit applications. Every agent/broker transacting basic property insurance in CA must either assist clients in securing FAIR Plan coverage or provide them the association's toll-free number.

In 2021, the CA FAIR Plan Association launched a clearinghouse program to reduce existing FAIR Plan policies by letting admitted insurers offer homeowners policies to FAIR Plan policyholders.

Basic property insurance (CIC 10091c) = direct loss coverage on real/tangible personal property at a fixed location, from perils under the standard fire policy plus extended coverage and vandalism/malicious mischief, plus any Commissioner-approved additions — excludes automobile and farm risks.

Personal Auto — Financial Responsibility & Good Driver Discount

🎧
Listen~1.9 min

1. Financial Responsibility Law (CVC 16020, 16021, 16025, 16451)

California's Compulsory Financial Responsibility Law requires every driver and every owner of a motor vehicle to maintain financial responsibility (liability coverage) at all times. Four forms satisfy it:

#Acceptable Form
1A motor vehicle liability insurance policy
2A $35,000 cash deposit with the DMV
3A surety bond for $35,000 from a company licensed to do business in California
4A DMV-issued self-insurance certificate

Drivers must carry written evidence of financial responsibility and show it to a peace officer after a citation stop or accident.

⚠️
Lacking acceptable proof can mean a fine or a vehicle impound. Lacking proof and being in an accident can cost the driver their license for up to 4 years.

2. Good Driver Discount — Eligibility (CIC 1861.025)

A person qualifies for a Good Driver Discount policy only if all of the following are true:

  • Licensed to drive a motor vehicle for the previous 3 years
  • During those 3 years, has not: had more than one violation-point count under the Vehicle Code (an at-fault accident causing property damage = one point count); had more than one dismissal of charges in a 36-month period for violations that would've caused more than one point count; or been the driver of a vehicle in an accident causing bodily injury or death, while principally at fault
💡
Eligibility based partly/entirely on driving experience acquired outside the US/Canada is presumed met if the person has been licensed in the US/Canada for at least the previous 18 months and satisfies the criteria above for that period.

3. Discount Amount & Rating Factors (CIC 1861.02, 1861.02a)

A Good Driver Discount policy's rate must be at least 20% below what the insured would otherwise be charged for the same coverage.

Rates/premiums for private passenger auto policies (PAPs) must apply these factors in decreasing order of importance:

  1. The insured's driving safety record
  2. The number of miles driven annually
  3. The number of years of driving experience

The Commissioner may adopt other factors with a substantial relationship to risk of loss; the weight given to each factor is set by regulation.

⚠️
The order is the trap: safety record outweighs annual mileage, which outweighs years of experience. Don't reshuffle this ranking.

4. Cancellation & Nonrenewal (CIC 1861.03c1, 661, 662, 663)

A notice of cancellation is only effective if based on one of these reasons:

  • Nonpayment of premium
  • The insured's driver's license or vehicle registration was suspended/revoked during the policy period, or during the 180 days preceding its effective date
  • Discovery of fraud in pursuing a claim
  • Discovery of material misrepresentation of: safety record; annual miles driven; years of driving experience; record of prior auto insurance claims; or any other factor substantially related to risk of loss
  • Substantial increase in the hazard insured against
⚠️
If the insured provides corrected information within 20 days of receiving the cancellation notice, the insurer must not proceed with the cancellation — but the insured still owes the premium difference for the period the misrepresentation went undisclosed.
🔢
Key numbers:
  • 3 years — licensing history required for Good Driver eligibility
  • 18 months — minimum US/Canada licensing for foreign-experience drivers
  • 20% — minimum Good Driver Discount
  • 180 days — look-back for a suspended license/registration to justify cancellation
  • 20 days — window to correct a misrepresentation and avoid cancellation

2. Uninsured Motorist Coverage — Offer or Reject (CIC 11580.2a1)

No auto liability policy for a vehicle principally used or garaged in California may be issued/delivered without uninsured motorist (UM) coverage. However, the coverage must merely be offered — the insured may reject it.

  • By written agreement, the insurer or any named insured may: delete the UM provision entirely; delete UM coverage when the vehicle is operated by a specifically named person; or reduce the amount of UM coverage.
  • If UM coverage is rejected on a policy, any continuation or renewal of that policy will not provide the coverage either.
⚠️
UM coverage is mandatory to offer, not mandatory to carry. The insured can reject it, delete it for a named excluded driver, or reduce it — all in writing — and once rejected, it stays rejected through renewals unless the insured affirmatively re-adds it.

🎧

  • SFP: drafted 1918, standardized 1943 as the NY Standard Fire Policy (46/48 states; MA used the New England Fire Policy, TX its own form). CA requires the standard form with no additions (CIC 2070), except a fire-only/combo policy at least as favorable to the insured.
  • SFP covers only 3 named perils: fire (hostile only, not friendly), lightning, and removal (5 days, all-risk, pro-rata) — the removal window temporarily covers even otherwise-excluded perils like theft.

  • 60+ days vacant/unoccupied excludes coverage — don't confuse with the 5-day removal window.
  • Amount payable is capped at the smallest of: policy limit, ACV, cost to repair/replace, insured's interest, or pro rata liability.
  • Residential disclosure (CIC 10101/10102): signature within 60 days of application; redisclosed every other year at renewal; no set-dollar-cap "guaranteed replacement cost" policies since 1/1/1993 (new) / 7/1/1993 (renewal); no-fire-coverage policies since 7/1/2021 need a 12-point bold notice; building code upgrade needs 10-point type.
  • Earthquake coverage must be offered on every residential policy (CIC 10081/10084) — never mandatory to buy. CEA sells only basic residential earthquake insurance, isn't backed by the CA Insurance Guarantee Association, and can surcharge up to an additional 20% of premium.
  • Condo earthquake loss assessment minimums: $50,000 (unit value >$135,000) / $25,000 (unit value ≤$135,000) — land value excluded.

Workers Compensation

The rating bureau and State Fund structure, employers liability coverage, and 24-hour coverage requirements.

I

Workers Compensation — General Concepts

🎧
Listen~1.6 min

1. California Workers Compensation Inspection Rating Bureau (CIC 11750.3)

The WCIRB is an unincorporated, nonprofit association comprised of all companies licensed to transact workers compensation insurance in California — it has over 400 member companies.

Its main functions:

  • Provide reliable statistics and rating information regarding Workers Compensation and employer's liability insurance
  • Collect and tabulate information/statistics to develop pure premium rates submitted to the Commissioner for issuance or approval
  • Inspect risks for classification or rate purposes and furnish this information to the insurer (and to the employer, upon request)

2. California State Compensation Insurance Fund (CIC 11784)

California operates the State Compensation Insurance Fund (State Fund / SCIF), but there is also open competition for workers comp insurance among commercial insurers.

💡
SCIF is now the insurer of last resort. Large employers with the financial ability to pay premiums are being turned away (or even asked to withdraw) from SCIF, unless they can show they can't obtain coverage in the commercial marketplace. Priority now goes to smaller employers with limited payrolls, regardless of whether they've shopped the open market.
Rate-Setting Factors

SCIF's Board of Directors sets the rates charged by the Fund. Rates must be fixed within each class of business, considering:

  • Bodily risk or safety, premises, or work hazard for each insured employer
  • The manner in which the work is conducted
  • A reasonable regard for the accident experience of each insured
  • A reasonable regard for the means and methods of caring for injured persons by each insured
⚠️
Rates may NOT take into account whether employees have persons dependent on them for support.

Very small employers are no longer required to prove that at least three insurers declined to cover them. Certain employers regulated under the U.S. Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act (rather than state WC law) may also obtain coverage from SCIF as well as commercial insurers.

Employers not eligible to self-insure must obtain the insurance. An employer who fails to do so is subject to a cease and desist order from the Department of Industrial Relations, closing the business until coverage is obtained.

🔢
The California Insurance Guarantee Association pays claims of insolvent insurers, but only limited resources are available to employees who should've been covered and weren't — and the employer is personally liable for such claims.

3. Employers Liability Coverage (CIC 11750.1f)

Under the compulsory Workers Compensation law, injured employees are barred from seeking damages outside WC law — the exclusive remedy doctrine. But some situations fall outside WC coverage, and Employers Liability coverage is designed for exactly those. Most common covered situations:

  • Exempt or illegal employment
  • Third-party-over claims — an employee successfully sues a third party, and that third party then sues the employer
  • Situations involving another relationship between employer and employee, called dual capacity

Example: an employee is instructed to make repairs using tools the employer manufactures, and is injured because of the tools. The employee can collect both Workers Compensation benefits AND additional compensation for being injured by a tool the employer manufactured.

  • Family loss of consortium, or loss of companionship, resulting from an employee's disability or death
  • Consequential bodily injury to an employee's family members, resulting from the employee's injury
  • Employees who attempt to seek benefits from parent-subsidiary relationships of the employer

Example: an employee is injured by a company (subsidiary) that is actually owned by another company (parent). The employee may collect Workers Compensation from the subsidiary AND seek additional compensation from the parent company.

4. 24-Hour Coverage (CIC 1749.02)

24-hour coverage provides employees with both a workers compensation policy and some type of medical insurance coverage (a disability income policy, health care service plan contract for injuries, etc.). Because the employee is covered at work by workers compensation, and during non-work hours by the medical plan, the worker is covered 24 hours a day.

💡
Same 2-policy structure covered in Ch. 3 §I — 24-hour coverage is never a single policy, and it may never be distributed using a life insurance policy.
II

Trap Summary

🎧
Listen~0.8 min
  • WCIRB is a nonprofit rating bureau (400+ member companies) — it sets pure premium rate data for Commissioner approval and inspects risks; it doesn't insure anyone itself.
  • SCIF is the insurer of last resort — large employers who can get coverage commercially are steered away from it; priority goes to small employers.
  • SCIF rates cannot factor in whether employees have dependents — but CAN factor in bodily risk/hazard, work manner, accident experience, and care methods.
  • Very small employers no longer need to show 3 insurers declined them before using SCIF.
  • Failing to obtain required WC coverage → a cease and desist order closing the business, plus personal employer liability for uncovered claims.
  • Employers Liability plugs the gaps the exclusive remedy doctrine leaves open: illegal/exempt employment, third-party-over suits, dual capacity, loss of consortium, family consequential injury, and parent-subsidiary claims.
  • Dual capacity example: hurt by a tool your employer manufactured → WC benefits AND product-liability-style compensation.
  • 24-hour coverage = WC policy + separate medical/disability policy, together — never a single policy, never via life insurance (same rule from Ch. 3).
