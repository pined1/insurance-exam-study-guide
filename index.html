I

Basic Insurance Concepts & Principles

🎧
Listen~1.2 min

1. Definition of Insurance (CIC 22)

💡
Insurance = transfer of risk of loss from an individual/business to an insurer, which spreads the cost among many insureds. Without insurance, the loss falls solely on whoever suffered it.

Statutory definition: insurance is "a contract whereby one undertakes to indemnify another against loss, damage, or liability arising from a contingent or unknown event."

Person (legal) = an entity acting on its own behalf, accepting legal/civil responsibility, contracting in its own name — individuals, associations, organizations, corporations, partnerships, and trusts.

2. Insurable Events (CIC 250)

"Any contingent or unknown event, whether past or future, which may damnify a person having an insurable interest, or create a liability against him, may be insured against."

⚠️
Predictability rule: the more predictable a loss, the more insurable it becomes; the more unpredictable, the less insurable. Gambling/lottery losses = NOT insurable.
⚠️
The law limits only the TYPE of insurable event — never the dollar amount. Level of indemnification is set by the parties in the contract.

3. Reinsurance (CIC 620)

One insurer indemnifies another for part/all of its liabilities. Purpose: protection against catastrophic losses.

Ceding insurerThe original company (it cedes/gives away the risk)
Assuming insurerThe reinsurer (takes on the risk)

4. Classes of Insurance (CIC 100–120)

20 regulatory classes exist. Key definitions for the exam:

Top traps — Part I:
  • "Transfer of risk" is THE one-line answer for defining insurance.
  • Liability's four exclusions: workers comp, common carrier, boiler & machinery, team & vehicle.
  • Ceding insurer (gives risk away) vs. assuming insurer (reinsurer, takes risk on).
  • Law caps the type of insurable event, never the dollar amount.
  • Ocean marine = sea import/export; inland marine = land/air movable property + pleasure watercraft hull.
  • Workers Comp = no-fault (irrespective of negligence).
  • Burglary excludes property with a carrier for hire or in the mail.
  • Earthquake appears under BOTH Fire and Miscellaneous.
II

Contract Law

🎧
Listen~1.2 min

1. The Insurance Policy (CIC 380)

The insurance policy = the written instrument setting forth the contract of insurance.

2. Six Required Specifications (CIC 381)

#Element
1The parties to the contract
2The persons/property insured
3Statement of insurable interest (if insured ≠ owner)
4The risks insured against
5The time period the policy is in force
6The premium (stated, or method of calculation if determinable only at termination)
⚠️
Trap: An insurer's financial rating is NOT required to appear in the policy — common "which is NOT required" question.

3. Key Legal Terms & Distinctions

TermDefinitionIntent required?Effect
RepresentationStatement believed true (not guaranteed) — insured's answers on applicationN/ACan be withdrawn/changed before policy effective, not after
MisrepresentationAn untrue representationNoVoid contract if material
FraudIntentional misrepresentation/concealment of a material fact to induce a contract or deceiveYesVoid contract; may be criminal
ConcealmentWithholding known material informationNo — voids regardless of intentMay void policy; rescission allowed
WarrantyStatement guaranteed true, becomes part of contract (express or implied)N/ABreach of material warranty → rescission; immaterial breach does not void policy unless specified
Materiality"Probable and reasonable influence" the fact would have on the party's decision—Determines if misrep/concealment/warranty breach voids contract
RescissionRevocation of the contract—Insurer refunds all premiums; insured loses claim rights
⚠️
Key distinctions to memorize:
  • Fraud = misrepresentation that is both material AND intentional. Misrepresentation alone ≠ fraud.
  • Concealment can void a policy even if unintentional — unlike fraud.
  • A representation can be withdrawn/changed only before the policy takes effect — not after.
  • CIC 354: a representation cannot qualify an express warranty, but CAN qualify an implied warranty.
  • Neither party must disclose personal opinions/judgment — only facts.
Warranty Details (CIC 440–449)
  • No special wording needed to create a warranty.
  • Express warranty = written in policy → automatically part of contract.
  • Implied warranty = unwritten, presumed from circumstances.
  • Created at or before execution; not limited by time (past, present, future, or combination).
Concealment — what does NOT need to be disclosed (CIC 330–339)
  • Known information / information that should be known
  • Information the other party waives
  • Information excluded by a warranty and not material to the risk
  • Information excepted from insurance and not material to the risk
  • Information based on personal judgment
Materiality (CIC 334)
💡
Disadvantages are always material information. Producers must disclose disadvantages (surrender charges, exclusions like war/terrorism/aviation/suicide, term length, premium increases, fees, substandard ratings) — not just advantages.

Example: concealing a recent heart attack has greater materiality impact than misstating age by 5 years — materiality is about impact on the decision, not the type of fact.

Representations & Misrepresentations (CIC 350–361, 780–784)

A representation is false when the facts fail to correspond with its assertions. A material misrepresentation would alter the underwriting decision if discovered. If material AND intentional → fraud.

4. Rescission (CIC 331, 338, 359, 447)

An injured party may rescind for:

  1. A false material representation (effective from when the representation became false)
  2. Concealment (regardless of intent)
  3. Violation of a material warranty or other material provision

Burden of proof: insurer must prove the omission was intentional AND done to obtain insurance fraudulently.

5. Fraud Penalties

Penal Code §550 (felony)CIC 782 (misdemeanor)
Jail/prison1 yr county or 2/3/5 yrs state prisonUp to 1 yr county jail
FineUp to $150,000 or 2× fraud value, whichever greaterUp to $25,000 (capped at 3× loss if loss >$10,000)

🔢
All claim/notice forms must carry CA's fraud-warning statement — except reinsurance policies (insurer-to-insurer transactions).

Health care fraud estimate: 3–10% of costs, conservatively >$53 billion/year (Coalition Against Insurance Fraud).

IV

Agents, Brokers & Life Settlements

🎧
Listen~1.1 min

1. Agents vs. Brokers

AgentBroker
RepresentsInsurerClient
Life/health?YesNo — no such license as "life broker"/"health broker"
Paid byCommission (insurer)Typically a fee from client
⚠️
Taking both a client fee AND an insurer commission on one deal = potentially unethical.

2. Life Settlements

Life settlement = selling a no-longer-needed policy to a third party for cash. Seller's life expectancy must be more than 1 year (distinguishes from viatical settlements, for the terminally ill).

TermDefinition
Qualified Institutional BuyerOwns/invests ≥$100 million in securities; SEC-permitted to trade unregistered securities
Life ExpectancyArithmetic mean of projected months to live, based on medical history/mortality
Life Settlement BrokerFiduciary to the owner only; excludes licensed providers, attorneys, accountants, financial planners
Financing EntityAccredited investor providing funds for settlement contract purchase, in writing
⚠️
NOT a life settlement contract: policy loans, bank loans, collateral assignments, agreements between close relatives, bona fide business succession, employer-owned key-employee insurance, service-recipient/provider agreements.
🔢
Licensing: 15 hrs CE + $171 fee (1-yr) — OR, if already a life agent ≥1 year: notify CDI within 10 days, pay $85 notification fee, renew biennially.

3. Acting Without Appointment

  • Insurer requires all agents appointed (captive) → unappointed agent can't solicit/submit applications.
  • Insurer doesn't require it → unappointed agent can present proposals; client payments go to the insurer, not agent; if a policy issues, insurer is deemed to have appointed the agent and must file notice with the Commissioner within 14 days.

4. Solicitors

  • Assists a P&C agent/broker with non-life/disability/health insurance.
  • Cannot simultaneously hold an agent/broker license.
  • Can work for multiple producers at once.
⚠️
"Life solicitor" and "accident & health solicitor" are NOT real licenses (CIC 1704) — heavily tested.

5. Agent's Errors & Omissions (E&O) Insurance

  • Covers negligent errors (done wrong) or omissions (not done). Highest-risk moments: sales interview & policy delivery.
  • Claims-made (modern trend) vs. occurrence (older, requires insured's consent for out-of-court settlement).
  • Deductibles usually $500–$1,000 per claim; renews annually.
⚠️
Not covered: criminal acts, unfair trade practices, material misrepresentation causing loss. If a claim results in a criminal conviction, E&O won't pay; agent stays personally liable.
V

Nonadmitted Insurers, Records, License Lifecycle & Ethics

🎧
Listen~1.2 min

1. Nonadmitted Insurers & Surplus Lines (CIC 703)

Nonadmitted insurer = not licensed/present in CA; represented only via surplus lines brokers.

🔢
Unlicensed acts on behalf of a nonadmitted insurer = misdemeanor + $500 fine + $100/month continuing violation.

2. Free Insurance Ban (CIC 777.1)

Illegal to give free insurance as an incentive for other business → up to 1-year suspension/revocation.

Exceptions: newspaper subscriptions, credit union shares, product-performance guarantees, title/life/disability insurance paying off debt on death/disability, attorney services, motor club services (towing, roadside, bail bond, DMV).

3. Names, Advertising, Records

RuleNumber
Max fictitious names per licensee2 (+2 more if acquiring another licensed business)
Address change noticeImmediately
Report convictions/admin actionsWithin 30 days of final disposition
License renewal cycle2 years; 60-day grace period (if paperwork/fee submitted by expiration)
License # size on materialsAt least as large as smallest address/phone on the doc

Disapproved name words include: advisor, analyst, assurance company, department, government, investment, national, state, trust, underwriter / affordable, cash, cheap, discount, elder, expert, legal, protection, secure, specialist.

4. Fiduciary Duty & MGAs

Diverting fiduciary funds = theft. Producer must remit premiums (minus commission) and hold funds in a separate trustee account.

MGA = negotiates ceding reinsurance / manages insurance business as insurer's agent.

⚠️
NOT an MGA: insurer's employee, US manager of an alien insurer's branch, underwriting manager not paid on premium volume, attorney-in-fact for a reciprocal insurer.

5. Appointment Rules

  • Insurer must file notice of appointment within 14 days; licensee becomes legal / insurer becomes liable as of the date the insurer signs.
  • Inactive license (no appointments) ≠ lost license — reactivates on next appointment.
  • Licensee may surrender license anytime (CIC 1708).

6. License Denial / Suspension / Termination

💡
No-hearing denial (exactly 4 conditions): felony conviction, misdemeanor insurance-law violation, license denial in past 5 yrs, license suspension/revocation in past 5 yrs.
  • "Convicted" includes nolo contendere (no contest) pleas.
  • All convictions must be disclosed, even if dismissed/expunged/probated — failure to disclose = automatic denial.
  • Cheating on the license exam → barred from exams/active license for 5 years.
  • License auto-terminates on death, org dissolution, or partner changes (unless filed within 30 days).

7. Continuing Education

  • 24 hours per 2-year term, incl. 3 hours ethics (Life, A&H, Property, Casualty, Personal Lines).
  • Multi-license holders: one set of CE satisfies all held license types.
  • Carryover: only 2nd-year hours carry over, capped at the renewal requirement.
  • Exempt: 30 continuous years licensed in CA and age 70+.
  • LTC training: 8 hrs/year for first 4 years, then 8 hrs/2-year term (counted within, not added to, overall CE).

8. Ethics

Client's interest first; competence; needs-based recommendations; plain language; confidentiality; obey the law; no unfair competitor remarks.

⚠️
Conflict of interest: recommending what benefits the agent's commission over the client, or what the agent wouldn't choose for themselves.
Prohibited: misrepresentation, twisting, concealment, diverting/commingling client funds (commingling is a violation regardless of intent).

VII

Highest-Yield Trap Summary

🎧
Listen~0.9 min
  • Insurance = transfer of risk — the one-line definition.
  • Fraud (material + intentional) vs. concealment (no intent needed) vs. misrepresentation (neither required, but must be material to void).
  • Fraud penalties: $150K/felony (Penal Code §550) vs. $25K/misdemeanor (CIC 782) — don't swap these.
  • The insurance code caps the type of insurable event, never the loss amount.
  • Liability insurance excludes: workers comp, common carrier, boiler & machinery, team & vehicle.
  • Ocean marine (by sea) vs. inland marine (land/air + pleasure watercraft hull).
  • Ceding insurer (gives risk) vs. assuming insurer (reinsurer, takes risk).
  • Agent represents insurer; broker represents client — no such thing as a "life broker" license.
  • "Life solicitor" / "accident & health solicitor" are NOT real licenses.
  • Surplus line broker is the only license type allowed to place business with nonadmitted insurers.
  • The four no-hearing license denial conditions: felony conviction, misdemeanor insurance-law violation, license denial in past 5 yrs, suspension/revocation in past 5 yrs.
  • Domicile = place of incorporation, never place of business.
  • Stock/nonparticipating (taxable stockholder dividends) vs. Mutual/participating (nontaxable policyowner dividends).
  • CE: 24 hrs / 2-year term incl. 3 hrs ethics; carryover only from the 2nd year, capped at the renewal requirement.
  • Appointment: insurer has 14 days to file notice; liability attaches on the signature date, not the filing date.

Life Insurance & Annuities

Policy replacement and cancellation rules, plus insurable interest.

I

Policy Replacement & Cancellation

🎧
Listen~1.8 min

1. Free-Look / Right to Cancel

Every individual life policy or annuity must state on the cover page/policy that the owner can return it within a set period for a full refund, no questions asked. Rescission means the policy is treated as if it never existed.

⚠️
Clock starts when the policy is received and signed for by the owner (Acknowledgment of Delivery Receipt) — NOT when the application was signed or the policy was underwritten.
TriggerFree-look periodRefund
Standard individual life policyAt least 10 days; insurer may extend up to 30 days (never less than 10)Full premium refund
Replacement policyMinimum 30 daysFull refund
Insured/annuitant age 60+30 daysFull refund
Variable life/annuity30 daysAccount value + policy fees (could be less than premium paid)
Insurer's deadline to refund after rescission notice—Must pay within 30 days
⚠️
Traps:
  • Free-look does NOT apply to term conversions or credit life policies.
  • The 10–30 day range is for standard policies; 30 days flat is required for replacements and age 60+ policies.
  • Variable products refund account value (can be less than premiums paid) — non-variable refunds are the full premium.
  • After the free-look period expires, cancellation may trigger a surrender charge (not automatically refundable).
Age 60+ Notice Requirement
  • Must appear on cover page/policy jacket in 12-point bold, with 1-inch margin on all sides, or on an affixed sticker.
  • Applies to individual life policies/annuities — excludes variable contracts and modified guaranteed contracts.
  • The phrase "known as a surrender charge" may be omitted if no surrender charges exist.

2. Replacement Rules (CIC 10509–10509.09)

Replacement = purchasing a new individual life/annuity contract that causes an existing policy with another insurer to be lapsed, surrendered, terminated, forfeited, converted (e.g., a nonforfeiture option), or have benefits reduced.

⚠️
Replacement rules apply whether the agent knows for sure OR should reasonably suspect replacement is occurring — suspicion alone triggers the duty.
RequirementDetail
Signed statements at applicationClient confirms intent to replace; agent signs a statement on their knowledge/suspicion of replacement
"Notice Regarding Replacement of Life Insurance"Separate disclosure, signed by client and agent at application time; often a triplicate form: client keeps a copy, one goes with the application, agent/branch may keep the third
New insurer → old insurerMust send a copy of the disclosure to the existing insurer
ConservationExisting insurer's attempt to "save" the policy by contacting the client — must be truthful; encouraged, done in the client's interest (not the old agent's)
Existing insurer's response window20 days to send the policyowner an outline of the current policy
Annuity ledger/summary requestExisting insurer must furnish within 5 working days of the request
Record retentionReplacing and existing insurers must retain signed apps/disclosures/materials for no less than 3 years
Free-look on the new (replacement) policy30 days, full refund, no questions asked
🔢
Memorize these time limits separately:
  • 20 days — existing insurer's conservation outline
  • 5 working days — existing insurer furnishing annuity summary/ledger
  • 30 days — free-look on replacement policies AND age-60+ policies
  • 3 years — record retention (not 2, not 5)
Replacement Rules Do NOT Apply To:
  • Credit life insurance
  • Group life insurance or group annuities
  • Same-insurer conversions/changes (internal policy change)
  • Replacing insurer = existing insurer (same-company transaction)
No-Agent Sales (direct response/internet)
  • Insurer must still present the replacement disclosure as soon as replacement is suspected, even if that means at policy delivery (e.g., via mail).
  • Must still ask on the application about existing life insurance/replacement intent.
  • Not using agents does not excuse replacement duties.

Burden of compliance falls on the replacing insurer — must ensure its agents comply, and must return incomplete applications/disclosures to the agent.

3. Agent Duty & Liability Pitfalls

⚠️
Never leave a client without coverage. If a client cancels/lapses the old policy before the new one is approved and then gets declined or rated on the new application, the client may become uninsurable.
  • If a client insists on canceling early against advice: get a signed letter stating the client is acting against the agent's advice (protects the agent from liability).

Replacement considerations to weigh (common distractor topics):

  • New policy may cost more (client is older, potentially less insurable)
  • Incontestability period may restart with the new insurer — check if the new company honors the already-met period
  • Premium guarantees may differ between policies
  • Surrender charges on the current cash-value policy — consider a 1035 exchange

4. Penalties for Replacement Violations

Violator1st Violation2nd+ Violation
AgentMinimum $1,000$5,000–$50,000 per violation
Insurer$10,000$30,000–$300,000 per violation
⚠️
Commissioner may suspend or revoke license of any violator. Trap: insurer penalties run roughly 10× the agent penalties at each tier — easy to swap the numbers.

5. Required Cover-Page Disclosures (CIC 10127.10)

Non-variable individual life/annuity policies need the standard 30-day/surrender-charge notice, in 12-pt bold, with 1-inch margins, on the cover page/jacket or a sticker.

⚠️
Excludes: variable contracts, modified guaranteed contracts, credit-transaction policies, contractual conversion/policy-change provisions, contributory/noncontributory employer group life or annuity contracts, and group term life.

Variable annuity/life/modified guaranteed contracts require an expanded notice covering: cancellation policy, investment restrictions, refund policy, and penalty/surrender charge.

6. Investment Handling During Free-Look (CIC 10127.10)

During the 30-day free-look for a variable annuity, the premium defaults to fixed-income/money-market investments unless the purchaser specifically directs it into the stock/bond portfolio underlying the contract.

⚠️
Refund consequence depends on where the money was placed:
  • Left in fixed/money-market (default) and returned within 30 days → refund of premium + policy fees (full).
  • Directed into stock/bond portfolio and returned within 30 days → refund of account value on the date received by the insurer/agent — could be less than the premium paid.
Biggest trap here: the variable-product refund is conditional on the investment choice, not automatically the full premium — opposite of the standard non-variable free-look, which always refunds the full premium.
II

Insurable Interest

🎧
Listen~1.6 min

Core Definition

💡
Insurable interest = a legitimate financial/relational stake in the continued life of the insured, required so insurance can't become a wager on someone's death. Insurance indemnifies; it does not allow speculation on another's life.
  • Anyone can insure their own life — no insurable interest issue (the owner controls the policy: increase, decrease, cancel, change provisions).
  • To insure someone else's life, you need insurable interest and that person's consent.

The 4 Test Questions

  1. Would the insured's death cause the policyowner (or dependents) financial hardship?
  2. Does the insured owe the policyowner money or economic support (current/future)?
  3. Do they share a close relationship with common economic/familial goals?
  4. Do they have common ownership interest in property?

Statutory List — Every Person Has Insurable Interest In:

#Interest
1Himself/herself
2Any person he/she depends on wholly or in part for education or support
3Any person under legal obligation for payment of money/property/services that could be delayed/prevented by death or illness (e.g., child support)
4Any person upon whose life an estate or interest vested in him/her depends

Memory Device: "Blood and Business"

CategoryIncludes
BloodFamily relations — spouse, parent/child, siblings; support one another or share common goals (e.g., home/business purchase)
BusinessBusiness partners/co-owners, employer–key employee, creditor–debtor (lender/borrower)
⚠️
"Blood" also covers former family relations — e.g., divorce decrees requiring the paying spouse to maintain life insurance on themself for alimony/child support. Ownership structure: the receiving spouse owns the policy on the paying spouse's (insured's) life.

Insurable Interest vs. Beneficiary Designation

⚠️
  • Insurable interest applies only to the policyowner, NOT the beneficiary.
  • Neither the policyowner nor the insured must show insurable interest when naming a beneficiary.
  • A beneficiary can be anyone — a stranger, business, or charity — as long as they're willing to accept the money.

Applies across insurance types generally (e.g., you can't insure a car you don't own — same ownership-interest logic).

⏰ Timing Rule — Highest-Yield Exam Trap

Insurance TypeWhen must insurable interest exist?
Life & HealthAt the time of application ONLY — not required to exist at the time of loss (death) (CIC 10110)
Property & Casualty (P&C)At both the time of application AND the time of loss
💡
This is the single biggest life-vs-P&C distinction tested: a life beneficiary's/owner's insurable interest can lapse (e.g., divorce) after issue and the policy remains valid, since interest existed at application. P&C coverage would not pay out if ownership interest is gone at the time of the loss.

Application note: if the relationship between insured and proposed owner isn't obvious (e.g., different last name/address), the agent should note the relationship on the application so the underwriter can verify insurable interest exists.

III

Highest-Yield Trap Summary

🎧
Listen~1.3 min
  • Free-look clock starts on delivery/signed receipt, never on application-signing or underwriting.
  • Standard free-look = 10–30 days; replacements and age 60+ always get a flat 30 days.
  • Variable product refunds = account value (can be less than premium); non-variable refunds = full premium.
  • Replacement disclosure duty triggers on mere suspicion, not just certainty.
  • Conservation protects the client, not the original agent's commission.
  • Time limits to keep straight: 20 days (conservation outline), 5 working days (annuity ledger), 30 days (replacement/60+ free-look & insurer refund deadline), 3 years (record retention).
  • Replacement penalties: agents $1,000 / $5K–$50K; insurers $10,000 / $30K–$300K — insurer tier is ~10× the agent tier.
  • Replacement rules don't apply to credit life, group life/annuities, or same-insurer conversions.
  • Insurable interest is required only for the policyowner, never the beneficiary — anyone can be named beneficiary.
  • Timing trap: Life/health needs insurable interest only at application; P&C needs it at application AND at loss.
  • "Blood and business" covers family and former-family (divorce/alimony) relations, plus business partners, key employees, and creditor/debtor pairs.
Chapter 3

Accident, Health & Senior Products

Workers comp & 24-hour coverage, medical expense insurance regulation, long-term care insurance, and Medicare supplement plans.

I

Workers Compensation & Medical Expense Regulation

🎧
Listen~1.7 min

1. Education Requirements (CIC 1749c)

Accident & health agents who want to sell 24-hour care coverage must complete an approved CE course on workers compensation and general principles of employer liability.

⚠️
Trap: required hours must be equal to, but NO GREATER THAN, the prelicensing hours required for a P&C broker-agent on the same subjects — a cap, not a floor. For resident licensees, this CE can count toward their regular CE requirement.

2. 24-Hour Coverage (CIC 1749.02)

24-hour coverage = an employee is covered by two separate policies together: a workers compensation policy (on-the-job injuries) plus a medical/disability policy (off-the-job injuries/illness) — combined, the employee is covered 24 hours a day, and the time/place of injury doesn't matter.

⚠️
24-hour coverage is never one single policy, and it may NOT be distributed using a life insurance policy.

Disadvantages: government regulation; difficulty coordinating WC benefits with the group/individual health plan; possible overlap between the two plans; confusion over deductibles, coinsurance, and copayments.

3. Medical Expense Insurance — Regulation of Providers (CIC 740)

💡
The Department of Insurance (CDI) is the default/primary regulator of any person/entity providing coverage for medical, surgical, chiropractic, physical therapy, speech pathology, audiology, mental health, dental, hospital, or optometry services — unless that provider can show another agency (federal, another state, or another CA agency) already has primary responsibility.

Health care services plans that stay under CDI include those run by nonprofit agencies, schools/colleges/universities, public agencies (for employees/students/faculty), and self-funded employer plans.

⚠️
Two-part exception: a nonprofit loses this CDI coverage only if it is both the insurance provider and the owner-operator of the care facility.
RegulatorPrimary jurisdiction over
CDI (Dept. of Insurance)Most PPO and EPO plans; other disability insurance companies
DMHC (Dept. of Managed Health Care)HMOs; Point of Service (POS) plans; some PPO/EPO plans
⚠️
Not all PPO/EPO = CDI — DMHC regulates some of them too. Because DMHC has primary enforcement authority over managed care, CDI's oversight there is only limited.
II

Long-Term Care Insurance — Core Rules

🎧
Listen~2 min

Pre-Existing Conditions

Pre-existing condition = a condition for which medical advice/treatment was recommended or received within 6 months preceding the policy's effective date.

⚠️
After the 6-month waiting period, the insurer must cover all disclosed pre-existing conditions, regardless of when the insured becomes disabled — unless permanently excluded or covered by a Commissioner-approved rider/waiver.

Free-Look / Return of Policy (CIC 10232.7)

🔢
30 days to return the policy by first-class mail for a full refund. The policy is void from the beginning — no claims payable.

Home Care Required Benefits (CIC 10232.8)

For tax-qualified policies, benefits trigger when the insured is chronically ill: impaired in at least 2 of 6 activities of daily living (ADLs), or has a severe cognitive impairment. Assessment must be by a health care practitioner who is NOT an employee of the insurer.

⚠️
"Impaired" = needs either substantial supervision (stand-by assistance) or hands-on assistance — either one counts.

Prohibited Provisions (CIC 10233.2)

  • Must be guaranteed renewable for life; can only be cancelled/nonrenewed for nonpayment of premium.
  • No new waiting period on a same-insurer replacement/conversion — except a benefit increase may get a temporary exclusion of up to 6 months.
  • Cannot limit benefits to skilled nursing only, use vague "usual and customary" language, or raise/cancel a spouse's coverage after divorce.
  • Additional specific benefit must be ≥5× the daily facility benefit.
  • Home/community care daily benefit must be ≥ the greater of $50/day or 50% of the facility-care benefit.

Replacement Policies (CIC 10233.3, 10234.95, 10234.16)

New contract cannot deny pre-existing conditions or restart an already-satisfied elimination period; insurer cannot reduce payment for expenses already paid out-of-pocket.

🔢
3 or more LTC transactions with the same insured within 12 months is not allowed.

Replacement notice to the applicant must cover: whether the decision could be a good one; that pre-existing conditions might not be covered immediately by the new policy; to check with the existing insurer; and that nondisclosure of medical history can void the contract (with full premium refund).

Outline of Coverage (CIC 10233.5)

⚠️
Must state that LTC is NOT Medicare supplement insurance, and that the cost of care rises over time but benefits do NOT unless an inflation-protection option is chosen.

Marketing Standards & HICAP (CIC 10234.93)

HICAP = Health Insurance Counseling and Advocacy Program — free help for consumers evaluating LTC policies. Insurers must disclose HICAP's existence, provide a current Area Agencies on Aging list, and give the Dept. of Aging's shopper's guide. Prohibited: twisting, high-pressure tactics, and undisclosed cold lead advertising.

III

Medicare Supplement & Long-Term Care Basics

🎧
Listen~2 min

1. Medicare Supplement (Medigap) Plans (CIC 10194.2–10194.3)

💡
Plan A = core/basic benefits only, and must be offered by every insurer selling Medigap. All other lettered plans are optional. Insurers cannot alter benefits within a plan or change its letter.

Core benefits (in every plan, incl. A): hospital co-pays days 61–90; lifetime reserve days 91–150; 365 extra days after Medicare is exhausted; first 3 pints of blood; Part B coinsurance (20%).

2. Guaranteed Issue & Anti-Discrimination

During open enrollment, the insurer must: sell the policy (guaranteed issue); cover pre-existing conditions after 6 months; not charge more for past/present health problems. (CIC 10194.8) bans unfair treatment of any person/class of risk in sale or pricing.

3. Medigap Replacement (CIC 10197)

  • No one may cause an unnecessary replacement (decreased benefits + increased premium).
  • Agent must list all other disability policies sold to the applicant — in force and those sold in the past 5 years even if lapsed.
  • Replacement notice (signed by applicant & agent) must be given before the new policy is issued; direct response insurers give a similar notice upon issuance instead.

4. Long-Term Care Insurance — Definition & Settings

Medicare pays for a max of 100 days in a skilled nursing facility (not 100% of cost). Medi-Cal LTC requires "spend down" and "asset recovery" after death.

LTC insurance covers diagnostic, preventive, therapeutic, rehabilitative, maintenance, or personal care services outside an acute care hospital unit (nursing homes, home health care, adult day care, hospice, etc.).

⚠️
Excludes: disability-based LTC policies, and anything designed primarily as Medicare supplement or major medical coverage.

LTC can be added as a rider to a Medicare supplement or health policy, but it never pays actual Medicare or major medical expenses.

IV

LTC Consumer Protection & Ethics

🎧
Listen~1.5 min

Duties of Honor, Good Faith & Fair Dealing (CIC 10234.8)

All insurers/brokers/agents owe LTC clients a duty of honesty, good faith, and fair dealing. Pre-purchase conduct during the offer/sale can be used as evidence in a legal action alleging a breach.

Unnecessary Replacement (CIC 10234.85)

🔢
Any 3rd or greater LTC policy sold to a policyholder within 12 months is automatically considered unnecessary — unless the sole purpose is consolidating policies with a single insurer.

Advertisement & Cold Lead Disclosure (CIC 10234.9)

RequirementDetail
Ad review by CommissionerAt least 30 days before use
Ad retentionAt least 3 years
⚠️
Lead-generating ads must prominently disclose that an agent will make contact. Anyone contacting a consumer from a cold-lead device must immediately disclose that fact.

Replacement Commission Rules (CIC 10234.97a–b)

Commission on a replaced LTC policy = calculated on the difference between the new and old annual premiums. If the new premium is ≤ the old one, commission is capped at the renewal rate (not first-year rate). Replacement requires the insurer to declare the new policy materially improves the insured's position. Does not apply to group insurance replacement. "Commission" includes any compensation — bonuses, gifts, prizes, awards, finder's fees.

V

Highest-Yield Trap Summary

🎧
Listen~1.3 min
  • 24-hour coverage = WC policy + separate health/disability policy, together — never one policy, never through life insurance.
  • CDI defaults as regulator for medical expense providers; steps back only when another agency's primary jurisdiction is proven.
  • CDI = most PPO/EPO + disability insurers; DMHC = HMOs, POS plans, and some PPO/EPO.
  • LTC pre-existing condition look-back = 6 months; LTC free-look = 30 days by first-class mail.
  • The 6 ADLs: eating, bathing, continence, dressing, toileting, transferring — impairment in 2 of 6 (or severe cognitive impairment) triggers benefits.
  • 3+ LTC transactions with the same insured within 12 months = automatically unnecessary replacement.
  • Medigap Plan A is mandatory for insurers to offer; Plans C, E, F, H, I, J are closed to new sales (still valid if already owned).
  • Medigap Plans K/L cover core benefits at 50% and 75% respectively — but days 61–90/91–150/+365 stay at 100%.
  • LTC excludes Medicare supplement and major medical coverage — even as a rider, it never pays those expenses.
  • LTC ad review: 30 days advance to Commissioner; 3-year retention — don't confuse with the 12-month/3-policy replacement rule.
  • Replacement commission: capped at renewal rate unless the new premium is actually higher than the old one.
